01

You want a system, and a blank page is not one

What clients ask

"Learn what I'm doing right and wrong. See if I can get to the next step in my journey to use Notion to manage my life."

"Understand what Notion does and how it will help me."

"Need help to structure."

What is really going on

Notion opens on an empty page and asks you to invent everything at once: the structure, the naming, the properties, the views. Most people build something ambitious in week one, find it too heavy to maintain by week three, and quietly stop opening it.

The cost is not the wasted evening. It is that you conclude Notion does not work for you when what failed was the blank page.

What I tell them

  • Start from a finished template that matches your use case. A good one covers about 80% of what you need on day one.
  • Delete before you build. Strip the sample data and any section you cannot name a use for, then rename the fields to your own words.
  • Add one thing per week. A simple table you actually open beats an elegant system you abandon.

Start from: Life OS for personal and home, Creative Studio for a small business. Both are free, and both are already structured so you edit rather than invent.

02

One place to look, instead of five tabs and a guess

What clients ask

"Current workflow spreads across Excel, HubSpot, and Nirvana, causing fragmented task, contact, and deal tracking."

"Disorganized Google Sheets and Docs, scattered image libraries, needing a clean, centralized system."

"Replace siloed Excel workflows with an interactive Notion setup."

What is really going on

Every extra tool adds one more place the answer hides. You stop trusting any single view, so you check all of them. That tax gets paid every day, and it gets worse as the team grows.

People then try to move everything into Notion in one weekend, run out of energy halfway, and end up with six tools instead of five.

What I tell them

  • Move one workflow first. Pick the one that hurts most, get it fully working, then move the next.
  • Import structured data with CSV. Notion reads a CSV straight into a database, which covers most spreadsheet migrations.
  • You do not have to move the files. Google Docs and Sheets embed live inside Notion with a working preview, so the file stays in Drive and the context lives in Notion.
  • Set a hard cutover date per workflow. Two systems running in parallel is the state that never ends on its own.

Start from: Creative Studio gives you projects, clients and tasks already linked, so the first workflow lands somewhere that scales. Free.

03

Everyone sees their own work, you see all of it

What clients ask

"Best way to assign tasks to team members, track project progress, and understand how permissions work for different users or teams."

"Scalable team system: shared and individual task management, project and event roadmap, progress and deadline tracking, weekly workflows, central hub."

"Info lost in team collaboration. Tasks disappear from the shared view."

What is really going on

The common failure is a separate task list per person or per project. Nothing rolls up, so the only way to know where a project stands is to ask three people.

The second failure is subtler. Someone edits a filter on a shared view, work vanishes from everyone's screen, and the team loses trust in the system within a week.

What I tell them

  • One Tasks database for the whole team. Never one per person, never one per project.
  • Give each person a filtered view of that single database, filtered on Assignee equals Me. Same data, personal screen.
  • Relate Tasks to Projects so status rolls up on its own and you stop chasing updates.
  • Lock the shared views once they work. View edits are the most common cause of "my tasks disappeared."

Start from: Creative Studio for a team hub, or Daily Weekly Monthly Planners if the weekly rhythm is what is missing. Both free.

04

Know who to follow up with today, without opening a spreadsheet

What clients ask

"Wants to build a CRM in Notion."

"Crear un CRM simple y escalable. Actualmente no tienen ningun sistema de gestion de clientes o leads."

"Optimize his recruiting business with a cleaner record of progress, notes, deals, and clients."

What is really going on

Contacts in a phone, deals in a spreadsheet, notes in an inbox. Nothing connects, so follow-ups depend on memory. Deals go cold for no reason anyone can point to.

The trap is building one giant table with forty columns. It is unreadable within a month and impossible to report on.

What I tell them

  • Use three databases, not one: Contacts, Deals, Activities. Relate them.
  • Put the pipeline stage on the Deal, not the Contact. One contact can have several deals, and stage history is what you report on.
  • Log every touch as an Activity linked to the Deal. Your follow-up list becomes a filtered view instead of a memory exercise.
  • Add a Next Action date on every open Deal. A deal with no next action is a deal you have already lost.

Start from: Ultimate Business Dashboard ships with clients, projects, deals and revenue already related. For a lighter start, the free CRM and sales trackers on my Notion Marketplace profile cover the same shape.

05

Enter it once, see it everywhere it matters

What clients ask

"Tasks not syncing correctly between project and main task databases."

"Rollup aggregations only fill one linked base, forcing double data entry."

"Connect client, meeting and activity tables via two-way relations. Consolidate duplicate client tables."

What is really going on

Without relations, the same client name gets typed into four tables. They drift, they get misspelled, and no number you produce can be trusted.

The usual cause of a broken rollup is a one-way relation, or a rollup pointed at the wrong property. It looks like a Notion bug. It is a wiring problem, and it is fixable in minutes once you can see it.

What I tell them

  • Make every relation two-way. Turn on "Show on" in the relation settings so both sides stay in sync.
  • Use rollups to read related values instead of copying them. Sum, count, latest date, all without duplicating data.
  • Merge duplicate tables before you build views on them. Deduplicating later means rebuilding every relation.
  • When you ask Notion AI to create a relation, name the exact target database. Vague prompts produce relations pointed at the wrong table.

Start from: Medical Practice is a compact worked example of related databases with working rollups. Free, and the pattern transfers to any industry.

06

Pay for the people who edit, not for everyone who reads

What clients ask

"Doesn't understand why he's paying for 3 Notion members when he only has 1."

"A $96 charge instead of the expected $24."

"Guests were auto-converted to paid members and he was billed."

What is really going on

This is the most expensive misunderstanding in Notion, and the most common. The people it hits are already paying. They are paying for seats nobody uses.

The cause is almost always the same. Everyone gets invited as a member, because member is the default, and every member consumes a paid license.

What I tell them

  • Know the difference. Members consume a paid license. Guests edit specific pages you share with them, and guests are free.
  • Audit Settings, then Members. Anyone who only needs a few pages should be a guest.
  • Remove the member first, then re-invite as a guest using Skip. Adding a guest without removing the member keeps the charge.
  • Act before the renewal date. Changes apply on the next cycle, and annual plans do not refund seats removed mid-term.

No template fixes this one. It is a 20-minute audit of your member list. If the charge is already wrong, contact Notion support before the next billing date rather than after it.

07

Give people exactly the page they need, and nothing else

What clients ask

"Share per-hotel training pages with 70 hotels without adding each hotel as a workspace member."

"Wants to share Notion lesson plans with her school, preferring publish-to-web links over PDF exports."

"Change member access levels so certain members are downgraded to guests."

What is really going on

People assume the only way to share is to add someone to the workspace. That is expensive, and it hands them a sidebar full of things they should not see.

So they export to PDF instead. The PDF is out of date the moment it is sent, and you end up maintaining two versions of everything.

What I tell them

  • Publish to web for read-only sharing with a wide audience. Anyone with the link can read it, and it updates itself.
  • Turn off search engine indexing on published pages unless you want them found. The link stays unguessable.
  • Use guest access when someone needs to edit. Granular, page-level, and free.
  • Share at page level, never workspace level, for external parties. One page in, nothing else visible.

Start from: Simple FAQ is built to be published and shared externally, so you can see the pattern working before you apply it to your own pages. Free.

08

The deadline finds you, instead of you finding the deadline

What clients ask

"Wants to use Notion to assign tasks and remind his team."

"Expiry-date reminders and compliance tables."

"Savoir si Notion peut prendre en charge les mails de confirmation ou de rappel personnalises et automatiques."

What is really going on

People assume a Date property sends an alert. It does not. Notion has no built-in due-date notification, so the date sits there and the deadline passes.

You then keep a second reminder system in your phone, which is the fragmentation problem arriving through a side door.

What I tell them

  • Use a Person property for assignment. Notion notifies the assignee natively when they are assigned, and that costs you nothing to set up.
  • Build the alert with a formula plus a database automation. A formula flags what is due soon, an automation acts on it.
  • Connect Slack through Notion Connections for two-way task notifications where your team already is.
  • Add a daily view filtered to Due this week. A view you open every morning outperforms a notification you learn to ignore.

09

A workspace that stays fast as your data grows

What clients ask

"Hit the 1000-block free-plan limit. Needed workspace restructuring to remove the limitation."

"Shared free-plan workspace hit the 1,000-block limit, blocking new pages."

"Concerns about Notion's performance with large databases and relation fields."

What is really going on

The 1,000-block limit is widely misunderstood. It applies only to free workspaces shared with two or more members. A private single-user free workspace has no block limit at all. People upgrade when they did not need to, or panic when they did not have to.

Slowness is a different problem, and it is rarely Notion. It is a table displaying thousands of unfiltered rows with twenty visible properties.

What I tell them

  • Check whether the limit even applies to you before upgrading. Shared and free is the only combination that triggers it.
  • Filter every large view and cap what is displayed to roughly 10 to 25 items. Nobody reads row 400.
  • Hide rarely-used properties. Move them to the page side panel instead of the table.
  • Keep one source database with many filtered views. Duplicating the table per segment multiplies the problem and splits your data.

No template fixes this one. It is a view-level cleanup and it usually takes under an hour. If your workspace is already large, the Notion Ops Audit is built for exactly this.

10

AI that produces something you can use, at a cost you control

What clients ask

"Notion agent for weekly timesheet summaries consumes credits quickly. Inconsistent PDF formatting. Needs to audit and streamline agent instructions."

"The AI-generated databases lack visual structure and usability."

"Streamline and automate their workflow and processes with AI."

What is really going on

People expect AI to design the system. It will not. Point it at a vague instruction and it produces a database that looks right and works badly, or it burns credits producing the same summary in a slightly different format every week.

The lesson clients take away: structure comes first. An agent built on a messy workspace produces messy output faster.

What I tell them

  • Know which tool you need. The chatbot answers questions and does bulk edits on demand. Custom agents run on a trigger or a schedule.
  • Name the exact database and the exact properties in your instruction. Precision is the whole game with AI in Notion.
  • Watch the credits. Agents and higher-tier models consume credits and need a paid plan. A weekly agent doing the work of a filtered view is money spent for nothing.
  • Connect Claude or ChatGPT through the MCP connector when you want external AI to act on your workspace.

What you get once these are fixed

None of these are Notion limitations. They are setup decisions, and every one of them is reversible.

You stop looking for things

One place holds the answer. The daily tax of checking five tools to find one number disappears, and it does not come back as the team grows.

You stop paying for seats nobody uses

Members edit, guests read, and guests are free. Most workspaces are overpaying, and the audit that fixes it takes 20 minutes.

Your numbers become trustworthy

Related databases and rollups mean data is entered once and read everywhere. No more four spellings of the same client name.

The system survives month three

Start from a template, cut what you do not use, add one thing a week. That is the difference between a workspace you keep and one you abandon.
