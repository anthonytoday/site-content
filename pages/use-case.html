The engagement

Seven findings on day zero.
Every one of them closed by day 21.

A new hire, added to a shared teamspace during onboarding, was able to view contract terms belonging to a different client. No breach occurred. The COO could not confirm who had access to what across the workspace.

Audit findings, rated by risk
  • Critical: 3 teamspaces set to open-edit with no access review. No control over who can modify client-facing content.
  • High: no offboarding process to revoke access when contractors or employees leave. A structural gap: it recurs with every departure.
  • High: 14 guest accounts still active from engagements closed 6+ months prior. Live, unreviewed access existing today.
  • Medium: 12 paid seats assigned to inactive or departed users. Recurring licensing cost with no corresponding usage.
  • Medium: no approval step before new databases go live. Unmanaged data sprawl over time.
  • Medium: 1 database with 40+ properties, no owner assigned. Reporting errors, duplicated manual work.
  • Low: no standardized templates for recurring project types. Inconsistent formatting, minor onboarding friction, no security exposure.
Day 21, after remediation
  • Access rebuilt on least-privilege principles, guest accounts reduced from 14 to 2
  • All open-edit teamspaces converted to role-based permissions with named owners
  • Offboarding checklist added to the HR termination workflow, tied to Notion admin access removal
  • Master database split into 3 purpose-built databases with defined ownership
  • Approval step added before any new database is created
  • 12 inactive paid seats identified and removed at the next billing cycle
  • Risk classification moved from High to Low (Acceptable)
Findings 2 and 3 are related. The missing offboarding process is the structural cause, the 14 live guest accounts are its current, visible consequence. Both were tracked and resolved separately.
Risk: High  →  Low (Acceptable)
How it ran

Audit first. Then remediation. Then a control that holds.

The company ran sales, product and client operations entirely in Notion.

Day zero

Notion Ops Audit ($197)

Teamspaces, guests, paid seats and database ownership reviewed. Seven findings, each rated Critical, High, Medium or Low, each with its business impact written down.

Days 1 to 21

Remediation

Access rebuilt on least-privilege principles: named owners, role-based permissions, an offboarding checklist tied to admin access removal, and an approval step before any new database goes live.

Day 21

Risk reclassified

Reduced to an acceptable level defined by three controls: every teamspace has a named owner, access is revoked automatically at offboarding, and permissions are reviewed quarterly rather than left indefinitely.
